# Design Links

## Design Set 1

1. [charitywater.org](https://www.charitywater.org): Full-bleed photography, plenty of white space and one bright accent color saved for the main action.
2. [givedirectly.org](https://www.givedirectly.org): A plain editorial layout that leads with evidence and impact numbers.
3. [kiva.org](https://www.kiva.org): A browsable grid of real people and their stories that makes one small action feel personal.
4. [savethechildren.org](https://www.savethechildren.org): One strong brand color, emotional hero images and a main action that's always in reach.
5. [doctorswithoutborders.org](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org): A news-led homepage built on field reporting that stays credible while asking for support.
6. [unicef.org](https://www.unicef.org): Clear country and language navigation for a worldwide audience.
7. [feedingamerica.org](https://www.feedingamerica.org): Simple impact math plus a local finder tool that turns a national number into a personal one.
8. [movember.com](https://movember.com): A playful campaign identity that makes taking part feel social and worth sharing.
9. [change.org](https://www.change.org): The petition pattern itself, with the signature form near the top, a live count and a progress bar toward a goal.
10. [secure.avaaz.org](https://secure.avaaz.org): Global petitions in many languages, with quick signing and member counts as social proof.
11. [globalcitizen.org](https://www.globalcitizen.org): Small actions (sign, share, email) that add up to a visible combined impact, aimed at young people.
12. [theoceancleanup.com](https://theoceancleanup.com): A cinematic scrolling story with big numbers and clear progress toward one huge goal.
13. [wikimediafoundation.org](https://wikimediafoundation.org): Calm, type-led, accessible design that works across many languages and scripts.
14. [foundation.mozilla.org](https://foundation.mozilla.org): A bold, flat graphic identity with petitions built into campaign pages.
15. [amnesty.org](https://www.amnesty.org): An instantly recognizable yellow-and-black identity with petitions and urgent actions up front.
16. [greenpeace.org/international](https://www.greenpeace.org/international/): Campaign hubs that combine strong photography, petitions and clear "get involved" paths.
17. [icanw.org](https://www.icanw.org): A Nobel Peace Prize–winning campaign whose pledges and treaty tracker make momentum visible.
18. [visionofhumanity.org](https://www.visionofhumanity.org): The interactive Global Peace Index world map, a model for showing signatures country by country.
19. [peaceoneday.org](https://www.peaceoneday.org): A whole campaign built around one idea and one date, 21 September, the International Day of Peace.
20. [nobelpeaceprize.org](https://www.nobelpeaceprize.org): A dignified, gallery-like presentation of laureates in a restrained palette.
21. [sfcg.org](https://www.sfcg.org): Warm human portraits and a story-led approach to conflict transformation.
22. [seedsofpeace.org](https://www.seedsofpeace.org): Youth-centered and hopeful, giving community voices room to speak for themselves.
23. [usip.org](https://www.usip.org): Research-heavy and institutional, useful for organizing deep content, reports and regional pages.
24. [peacekeeping.un.org](https://peacekeeping.un.org): The official UN blue system, showing how peace looks at a global, institutional scale.
